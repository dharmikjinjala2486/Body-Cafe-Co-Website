# Variant E: Seven supplements. One colour each.

Creative research and page-by-page direction · 31 October 2026

## Recommendation

Develop Variant E into **The Living Fan Deck**: a recognizable collection of seven coloured objects that becomes a useful browsing system throughout the site. Keep the headline **“Seven supplements. One colour each.”** exactly. Retain the existing wordmark, Aileron and Lemon Milk, cap palette, white/cream surfaces and clean geometry.

The design opportunity is to make colour perform a different useful job on each page. On Home it introduces the collection. In Shop it organizes the range. On a product page it identifies one product. In Learn it connects an explanation to that product. In Reports it connects the selected product to its lot. In the bag and account it helps someone recognize what they chose.

**“Super Seven” is a possible campaign label, not the replacement headline or a new brand.** Use it sparingly for an introductory editorial or campaign. It should never suggest taking all seven, a seven-day treatment, seven promised outcomes, or a clinically superior range.

This document is research and original proposed art direction. It does not apply a redesign, change the backend, publish the website, or promise award recognition.

## What was examined

- Read-only browser audit of **74 customer URLs**, including all seven product pages, all twelve Learn guides, all 23 Help articles, both bundle details, all five policies, account routes, search, reports and transactional layouts. Populated order/payment/checkout layouts were examined using labelled fixtures. Protected account routes were observed signed out; their signed-in layouts and operations surfaces were reviewed in source.
- Source review of E’s theme, shared page renderers, content models and route dispatch. Audit observations are saved in `page-audit.json` and `help-audit.json` next to this report; `help-inventory.json` records the article coverage.
- Primary reference material from Awwwards, The Webby Awards, design studios and the brands themselves. Awwwards pages that timed out in text browsing were verified in the browser instead. Garoa’s listing identifies its educational/masterclass origin; treat it as an award-recognized design reference, not proof of a commercial conversion result.
- Historical award versions and current live brands are distinguished. A new live site may differ from its award entry. These are selected references, not an objective ranking of every website in the world.

## The current E audit

| Surface | What is working | Opportunity found |
|---|---|---|
| Home | Distinct headline, cap tray, interactive fan deck, seven-colour identity | Desktop headline takes much of the first screen; the full product system arrives later. Connect the headline directly to a memorable physical action. |
| Shop | Consistent product cards, actual formats, useful filter/sort, cap bands | Strong foundation. More distinction will come from object framing and concise facts, rather than extra sections below the grid. |
| Product pages | Eight consistent sections, label facts, gallery, purchase controls | Roughly 900–1,100 visible words per product in the audit. Repetition competes with the product’s one most useful distinction. |
| About | Genuine assets and values; transparent limits | Eight sections, a heavy uppercase hero and a visible People placeholder. Give it a human point of view and remove empty destinations from the customer journey. |
| Story | Own packaging, cap, logo and type assets; hidden unsupported founder history | Currently overlaps About and Standards. Make it a visual account of real design decisions. |
| Standards | Evidence states, product picker, clear explanations | About 2,000 visible words and ten major sections. Put status and records first; reveal the method when requested. Some “awaiting labels” lists are stale against the newer product facts. |
| Learn hub | Seven product guides, five foundations, search and glossary | About 1,450 visible words and nine major sections before opening an article. A publication front page would make it easier to enter. |
| Learn articles | Sources and structured explanations | Product guides have many repeated headings and overlap PDP facts. Give each article one clear learning purpose and a distinctive diagram. |
| Reports | Product-first lookup, document/assessment/notice separated | Valuable differentiation. The lookup should lead; explanatory material should support a result, rather than surround the task. |
| Bundles | Small, readable, explicitly separate from a regimen | Detail pages are very thin. Explain exact contents, price arithmetic and selection intent before adding campaign spectacle. |
| Cart, checkout, account | E colours and consistent transaction controls | Keep the brand in the objects and typography; simplify the surrounding content. These pages need confidence and recovery, with very restrained motion. |
| Help, Contact, policies | Existing route coverage and contextual links | Distinguish answers, case creation and full policy documents. Remove launch placeholders only when the underlying facts are approved. |
| Global shell | Original logo, recognizable stripe, consistent links | Make the cap-to-product mapping persistent, but give it text labels. Standardize editorial headline treatment across Home/About/Learn. |

These are art-direction observations, not measured usability or conversion findings. Word totals are approximate DOM text counts, not formal reading-time estimates. No claim is made that shorter is automatically better; improve the order and presentation of essential information.

## Three hero concepts, all within E

### 1. The Living Fan Deck — recommended

**Opening frame:** the unchanged headline is visible immediately alongside seven top-down caps, the complete colour order and a clear Shop CTA. A static first frame already communicates the concept.

**Reveal:** the caps lift or tilt into a measured, shallow fan of the seven actual bottles. Movement comes from the bottle object, with a restrained camera and the existing white/cream world. The full collection remains visible; one object can receive attention without erasing the others.

**Selection:** focus, hover or tap brings one swatch forward. The product name, format and one factual label detail appear in a fixed information position. A second deliberate action opens its page. The CTA never moves away from the pointer or disappears during animation.

**Handoff:** the selected swatch aligns with the product page’s cap band. A short transition makes the next page feel like opening that object’s file. On mobile, one tap selects and an explicit “View product” button navigates; seven readable choices remain available.

**Why it wins:** it develops the existing fan deck, makes the headline tangible, works with current bottle assets and creates a reusable interaction for the whole site. It has room for craft without requiring a new visual theme.

**References:** R2 for scene-to-shopping continuity, R3 for object-derived controls, R5 for a consistent shape language. The exact seven-cap choreography is an original BCC proposal.

### 2. Seven in Formation — a campaign or alternate hero

Seven bottles form a precise sculptural composition loosely suggesting the numeral 7. A gentle opening movement separates them into a clear product lineup, with the original headline unchanged. On compact screens the lineup is the default, rather than a tiny hard-to-read sculpture.

This has a stronger campaign silhouette and could support “The Super Seven” as a small editorial label. It needs careful composition so bottles and labels remain recognizable. The reference principle is R3’s connection between physical product and digital presentation. Use this as a photo/motion campaign if the living fan deck is the main site hero.

### 3. Open a Colour — the lowest-risk refinement

Seven vertical swatches stand beside the headline. Selecting a named colour opens a cream panel with the actual bottle, price and one label fact. The interaction starts in the hero rather than being postponed to the next section.

This uses E’s existing mechanism most directly and is the best fallback if the new bottle choreography does not meet performance or readability goals. Unlike the hero and collection repeating the same action, the lower collection would then become a factual grid or selected comparison.

### Hero choice

Build one hero system, not three competing homepages. My recommendation is **Living Fan Deck**, with **Open a Colour** as its static/reduced-motion behavior. Reserve **Seven in Formation / Super Seven** for campaigns and social assets. Preserve the headline as actual readable text through every state.

## Page-by-page creative brief

### 01 · Home `/`

**Job:** make the seven-colour idea memorable and help someone enter the range.  
**Proposed creative title:** keep **Seven supplements. One colour each.**  
**References:** R2, R3, R5.

Proposed sequence:

1. Living Fan Deck hero, with the whole range, headline and Shop action in the first composition.
2. A browseable collection: one factual identifier per product, clear price and availability. Avoid repeating the hero’s entire interaction.
3. **One colour. One useful detail.** Seven short visual explanations; choose a cap to inspect its label distinction.
4. **Behind the colour.** Three compact evidence destinations: label facts, manufacturing details, lot records. Status text is attached to the record it describes.
5. **Your bottle has a reference.** A concise product/lot lookup.
6. An editorial invitation into Learn. If there are no real published reviews, this is a better use of space than a large empty customer-voices section.
7. A short FAQ and one clear next step.

Retain useful functionality and the familiar overall rhythm. A new section order should be validated against the existing homepage requirements before implementation. There should be no fake rating, automatic full-range basket or claim that seven products form one regimen.

### 02 · Shop `/shop`

**Job:** choose and compare without work.  
**Concept:** **The colour index.**  
**References:** R4, R12; R2’s documented filters.

Keep the grid as the default; it already works. Add a compact named colour rail that filters or moves to the corresponding product with clear selected state. Every card keeps the same order: number, product name, bottle, short factual line, pack, price and action. All seven do not need a different card layout.

Offer optional comparison for up to three products, limited to objective fields. A “View differences” mode should compare format/serving/pack/allergens, not choose a supplement for a medical condition. Filtering and sorting must preserve the product’s assigned colour and number.

**Signature detail:** a selected chip slides into a small comparison shelf; the grid remains stable. On mobile use a compact comparison summary and readable stacked facts.

### 03 · Product detail `/products/:id` — one family, seven personalities

**Job:** understand this product and buy with clear information.  
**Concept:** **One colour, in detail.**  
**References:** R3, R8, R12.

Keep E’s cap-band-over-cream composition. The first screen should show the actual bottle, product name, format, pack count, serving basis, price, availability and quantity/add control. Use one strong informational visual below it instead of eight equally loud panels.

Recommended structure: purchase → defining label detail → readable full facts → use/cautions → evidence and lot lookup → genuine reviews/Q&A → other products. Sticky local navigation can expose facts/use/reports without presenting a long table of contents at the top.

The following distinctions come from the existing supplied label records. These are proposed visual explanations, not new benefit claims:

| Product | Proposed editorial hook | Signature visual | Critical detail |
|---|---|---|---|
| Iron Bisglycinate | **The iron number, clearly stated.** | Terracotta typographic composition centered on 29 mg, with compound/form named underneath | Identify the 29 mg as iron, not the mass of the entire chelate. Keep the serving basis and cautions adjacent. |
| L-Theanine | **200 mg. A clear starting point.** | Olive number/label study, with the capsule serving and 30-unit pack revealed through a clean comparison | No tea-source illustration that implies a verified commercial ingredient origin. |
| Vitamin D3 + K2 | **Three nutrient entries. One softgel.** | Three indigo information windows for D3, K2 and Omega-3; open each to show its label value | Preserve different units. D3 600 IU, K2 55 mcg and Omega-3 200 mg are not quantities to sum. |
| Omega-3 Fish Oil | **Read inside the 1,000 mg.** | Nested blocks for fish oil, total Omega-3 and its named EPA/DHA components | 800 mg Omega-3 includes EPA 400 mg and DHA 300 mg. The remainder is not a newly identified ingredient. |
| Myo & D-Chiro Inositol | **A ratio you can read.** | A lilac 40:1 diagram, then the two stated masses in readable text | Keep 2,000 mg + 50 mg tied to a three-capsule serving. A ratio must not imply an outcome. |
| Creatine Chews | **Two tablets. The full details.** | Teal 2 → 3,000 mg typographic transformation; orange flavour noted as text/detail | These are chewable tablets, not gummies. Sugar disclosure remains visible; no invented tablet appearance or sugar-free implication. |
| Psyllium Husk | **Husk and fibre, separately.** | Sand-colour nested information blocks for husk, dietary fibre and soluble fibre | Keep 2,000 mg husk separate from 1,578 mg dietary fibre and its 1,362 mg soluble component. Water directions stay easy to find. |

For every PDP, labels and values remain readable without animation. Use real package assets; a staged laboratory, fake certification seal or invented supplement-unit close-up would undermine the concept.

### 04 · Bundle listing `/bundles`

**Job:** understand a merchandising edit.  
**Concept:** **Colours, considered together.**  
**References:** R4 and R1.

Show each edit as an arrangement of its actual two product colours on a cream shelf. Present contents, formats, total and any approved saving together. Clearly label the edit’s merchandising purpose. Avoid health-result pairings and language that recommends combining products. If bundle rules are not approved, a comparison/edit page is more honest than a forced upsell.

### 05 · Bundle detail `/bundles/:id`

**Job:** know exactly what this edit includes.  
**Concept:** **Two colours. Every detail.**  
**References:** R12’s aligned comparison and R3’s product-first storytelling.

An initial paired still life opens into two side-by-side product fact sheets. State quantities, individual prices, bundle total and saving only when real. Include individual-product links and the full serving/allergen distinction. Show a single clear Add edit action after those facts. On mobile the sheets stack; the shared total remains visible. Do not hide one product’s cautions behind the other.

### 06 · Search `/search` and the search dialog

**Job:** reach the right product, guide or support answer.  
**Concept:** **Find the name. Follow the colour.**  
**References:** R2’s search elements and R15.

Search suggestions split into Products, Learn and Help. A colour dot identifies a product match; a guide can show the same product chip where relevant. Display exactly why an item matched: name, ingredient or format. Keep search query, results count and a clear empty state. For misspellings, offer a visible suggestion rather than silently changing the query.

### 07 · About `/about`

**Job:** explain who Body Café Co. is today and why its approach is worth attention.  
**Concept:** **Seven colours. One point of view.**  
**Primary references:** R7 Aesop’s editorial philosophy, R6 Secrid’s purpose/object connection, R5 Mollie’s shape system.

This is the most important supporting-page change. Replace a generic brand introduction with an editorial sequence:

1. One large, calm point-of-view statement beside a beautiful crop of the actual seven-cap system.
2. **Why colour?** Reveal name → colour → format → readable label. Demonstrate recognition rather than asserting design quality.
3. **How we behave.** Keep the existing four values, each shown through a real action: readable facts, useful questions, honest evidence status, a followable support case.
4. **The people, when ready.** Genuine portrait, name, role and a short first-person note. Until supplied, remove the empty People chapter and its CTA rather than leaving a promise in the main journey.
5. A compact bridge to Story, Standards and the range.

**Signature interaction:** seven cap circles align into the horizontal colour index as the point of view resolves. Motion is subtle and leaves the original wordmark untouched. The headline uses E’s editorial lightness; Lemon Milk remains available for product identifiers instead of dominating every prose page.

### 08 · Story `/story`

**Job:** explain how the brand’s choices developed, using genuine material.  
**Concept:** **How seven became a system.**  
**References:** R3, R4 and R8’s separation of founder story from proof.

Use an object table rather than a fictional founder biography: a colour sheet, cap, bottle label, type specimen and wordmark. Each object opens a short account of a real decision. A scroll sequence can move from a single cap to the complete collection, with close-up images and short captions.

The current genuine assets can support a design story now. A founder-led origin story needs approved first-person material, dated milestones and authentic imagery. Do not create a chronological history from undated files. Avoid repeating About’s values or Standards’ evidence definitions.

### 09 · Standards `/standards`

**Job:** show what can be checked and where the supporting record lives.  
**Concept:** **Behind every colour, a record.**  
**References:** R8 sourcing, R10 evidence navigation, R11 process stages.

Start with a seven-product selector and a compact status sheet: label facts, named manufacturer, ingredient-origin evidence, testing documents and lot record. The product colour identifies the selected product; explicit text identifies the evidence state.

Follow with a visual chain: ingredient → finished product → lot → document. Every stage explains what its source establishes, and what remains unavailable. Keep the detailed methodology as expandable reading below the practical record. Replace stale “labels missing” copy with a status sourced from the same product records as the PDP.

**Signature detail:** selecting a colour updates one stable record sheet. Do not invent seven quality promises simply to match the number seven. Product colour must never stand in for pass/fail or certification.

### 10 · Learn hub `/learn`

**Job:** help a reader choose a useful question.  
**Concept:** retain **A little more knowing.**  
**References:** R9 Seed Cultured, R15 information hierarchy.

Treat this as a publication front page: one featured question, seven ingredient guides, five foundation guides, a glossary entry point and useful search. Reduce the repeated card clusters and long secondary sections. Give editorial cards a clear title, short premise and topic. Show author/reviewer/date only when those records exist.

**Signature detail:** a book-spine-like colour index connects seven ingredient guides. Foundations stay neutral so a general explanation is not accidentally assigned to one product. Keep education readable and open without account creation.

### 11 · Seven ingredient guides `/learn/:product`

**Job:** explain the ingredient, not repeat the whole product page.  
**Concept:** **One ingredient. A better question.**  
**References:** R9 article structure and R3 visual specification teaching.

Each guide needs a short answer, one diagram, a carefully sourced explanation, a clearly separated “Inside our product” panel, questions and sources. Preserve the boundary between general ingredient research and BCC-specific evidence.

| Guide | Lead educational question | Visual treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Iron | What does elemental iron mean on a label? | Named compound versus labelled iron amount; no absorption superiority claim. |
| L-Theanine | What does the full ingredient name tell you? | Ingredient identity and stated serving; tea occurrence separated from verified supply origin. |
| D3 + K2 | Why are these values written in different units? | Three labelled value windows; no numerical sum of IU/mcg/mg. |
| Omega-3 | How are fish oil, Omega-3, EPA and DHA related? | Nested quantities with exact inclusion labels. |
| Inositol | How do you read a 40:1 ratio? | Ratio diagram plus serving basis, not a therapeutic recommendation. |
| Creatine | What changes when the format is a chewable tablet? | Serving count, labelled compound amount, other ingredients and sugar disclosure. |
| Psyllium | Why is husk weight different from fibre content? | Nested amounts; preparation/cautions linked to the actual label. |

The diagrams are communication proposals based on existing label facts. New scientific statements need their own source review before publication.

### 12 · Five foundation guides `/learn/:topic`

**Job:** teach one transferable reading skill.  
**References:** R9 and R15.

| Existing guide | Proposed visual tool | Exit action |
|---|---|---|
| Reading a label | An annotated schematic label with numbered zones | Open an actual product’s facts |
| Understanding reports | An explicitly illustrative report, separating result/unit/method/limit | Look up a lot |
| Ingredient forms | Compare the same type of labelled value with form names intact | Read an ingredient guide |
| Serving sizes | Select a known example and show units per pack ÷ units per serving | Check a product’s serving basis |
| Understanding evidence | Ingredient research / product study / batch testing / customer experience as separate categories | Open Standards |

Use fictional examples only when clearly labelled as examples. Do not let an illustrated report look like a real BCC laboratory document. A serving-size calculator explains arithmetic; it does not recommend a dose.

### 13 · Lot reports `/reports` and stable record links

**Job:** identify the matching record and communicate its status.  
**Concept:** **Your colour. Your lot. Its record.**  
**References:** R8/R10 evidence organization, R15 focused information.

Put the seven named product chips, lot field and lookup action first. After submission, present a product-coloured record header, exact lot, document state, assessment state and notice state. Let readers expand report details and definitions after seeing the summary.

A scan/open-from-pack shortcut can be a later convenience when real labels carry stable URLs. Always offer manual entry; a QR route is not proof of authenticity. Missing, unpublished, withdrawn, no-match and unavailable states need different messages and clear next actions.

### 14 · Bag `/cart`

**Job:** confirm choices and totals.  
**Concept:** **Your colours, gathered.**  
**References:** R1’s documented cart; R2’s documented empty-bag element.

Use small bottle thumbnails and coloured tabs as identifiers, with readable names, pack counts, quantities and prices. A compact strip of the selected colours can be the brand signature. It represents selections, not a recommended stack. Keep totals, delivery, discounts and change notices explicit.

Empty state: a few open shelf positions and “Your bag is waiting for a colour,” followed by Shop all seven. Do not turn emptiness into a long upsell. Keep undo after removal and preserve the shopper’s context.

### 15 · Checkout `/checkout`

**Job:** complete the order accurately and with confidence.  
**Concept:** **Clear details. Clear next step.**  
**References:** R13 guest checkout, R14 forms/recovery.

Give delivery details and a stable order summary priority. Keep Guest checkout explicit. Group optional fields such as GST or marketing away from required delivery fields; keep the price calculation visible before the final action. Product colours appear in the summary, not as a decorative background behind fields.

For Indian delivery, allow real address complexity without forcing a fake auto-filled state or unsupported serviceability promise. Preserve form input after validation errors and show an error beside the affected field plus a useful summary. This page’s craft is restraint.

### 16 · Payment `/payment/:id`

**Job:** communicate the verified payment state and safe next action.  
**Concept:** **A clear answer, even when payment is unclear.**  
**Reference:** R14.

Pending, paid, failed, uncertain, expired and unavailable each get a clear heading, amount/order reference and one primary action. Preserve the order on retry. An uncertain result says that the status is being checked; it should not encourage repeated payment immediately. Only a verified success receives a brief confirmation motion. The seven-colour motif must not impersonate financial status.

### 17 · Order detail `/orders/:id`

**Job:** answer “where is it, what happens next, and how can I get help?”  
**Concept:** **Follow your colour home.**  
**References:** R15’s hierarchy and R14’s explicit states, adapted as our own order-service proposal.

Lead with the current state, the next step and real delivery/tracking information when available. Then show ordered items, a concise timeline and linked lot records. Keep address, invoice and support accessible. A refund or cancellation can diverge into its own clear timeline; do not force every state through a cheerful four-step sequence.

### 18 · Sign-in `/sign-in`

**Job:** verify access with little effort.  
**Concept:** **Back to your colours.**  
**References:** R13 and R14.

Use a compact mobile-number/code interface, whole-code paste, clear resend timing, change-number control and a readable error. Say exactly why signing in is useful. Keep the guest shopping path available. Any decorative seven-cap composition stays secondary and does not move the input or keyboard focus.

### 19 · Account overview `/account`

**Job:** return to the active task.  
**Concept:** **Your shelf.**  
**References:** R4’s connection beyond a purchase; R15’s task hierarchy.

An active order or support case comes before generic account tiles. A small shelf of previously bought product colours helps recognition. Empty state guides the first purchase without fabricated personalization. Never infer a health condition from purchase history.

### 20 · Account Orders `/account/orders`

**Concept:** **Every order, easy to find.**  
**Reference:** R15.

Show order date/reference, plain status, item-colour markers and total. Support order search/filter only when volume justifies it. Reorder must check current price and stock rather than silently replay an old order. Keep receipts and lot links attached to the appropriate order.

### 21 · Addresses `/account/addresses`

**Concept:** **One less thing to type.**  
**Reference:** R14.

Use readable address cards with explicit default, edit and delete actions. Good small-screen formatting matters more than a new hero. Confirm which address an order will use; changing an account address must not imply that an already-placed order changed.

### 22 · Support list and case detail `/account/support` and case routes

**Concept:** **One case. The whole conversation.**  
**Reference:** R15.

Lead each case with a reference, topic, status, latest reply and next required action. Keep public conversation distinct from private staff notes. Product chips carry context when relevant. A priority safety case gets clear handling language; it must not be treated as a review request or sales opportunity.

### 23 · Reviews `/account/reviews`

**Concept:** **Your experience, in your words.**  
**References:** R4’s community principle; R15’s task clarity.

Separate items eligible for review, saved/submitted reviews and moderation states. Show the actual product and order context. Explain pending publication plainly. Avoid review rewards or promises of publication unless a policy and backend flow exist.

### 24 · Profile and security `/account/profile`

**Concept:** **Your details. Your control.**  
**References:** R14 and R15.

Prioritize current contact details, verified status and session controls. A number change or deletion should describe the exact consequences and recovery route. Keep medical details out of a basic shopping profile. Use the E typography and small cap motif, with no elaborate animation.

### 25 · Preferences `/account/preferences` and `/settings`

**Concept:** **Only what you choose.**  
**Reference:** R16 for readable policy navigation; preference behavior is our own proposal.

Separate necessary functions, optional analytics and optional communications. State whether a choice applies to this browser or an account, show a save confirmation and avoid ambiguous toggle labels. The seven colours represent products; they do not become seven decorative consent switches.

### 26 · Help hub `/help`

**Job:** find the answer or right support route.  
**Concept:** **Good questions welcome.**  
**Reference:** R15, with Ritual’s separate help destination as a category-specific cross-check.

Use a prominent task search, a few common actions, then Orders, Delivery, Products, Returns and Account topics. Display the answer in plain language before asking someone to create a case. Promote a contextual “Still need help?” route that carries the article/topic into Contact.

### 27 · Help article `/help/:category/:slug`

**Concept:** **The answer first.**  
**Reference:** R15.

Lead with a one-paragraph answer and the exact steps. Keep related questions and relevant policy links below. Include a short feedback prompt and a case action that preserves context. Avoid giving every help article a tall promotional hero.

### 28 · Contact `/contact`

**Job:** get to the correct person or case.  
**Concept:** **Pull up a question.**  
**Reference:** R15’s task-based routing, adapted to the brand’s welcoming voice.

A short topic choice reveals only the necessary fields. A product topic can offer the seven named colours; an order issue can accept an order reference. Confirm the case ID and where replies will appear. Clearly list real channels and hours when known. “Café” supplies warmth in the language; it does not require coffee-shop graphics or a fictional physical café.

### 29 · Shipping `/policies/shipping`

**Concept:** **Before it reaches you.**  
**References:** R16’s document navigation; R14’s visibility of policies during checkout.

Put approved regions, charges, processing and tracking in a concise summary. Full terms follow with stable headings. A pincode tool should report real serviceability only after integration. Do not animate delivery milestones as though they are promises.

### 30 · Returns and refunds `/policies/returns`

**Concept:** **If something isn’t right.**  
**Reference:** R16.

Show the main request categories, time limits, item condition and next step in plain language, followed by the full approved rules. Distinguish damaged/wrong/expired items from change-of-mind. Keep actual refund status attached to the order/case, rather than implying that submitting a form completes a refund.

### 31 · Cancellations `/policies/cancellation`

**Concept:** **A clear way to change course.**  
**Reference:** R16.

A simple before-dispatch/after-dispatch explanation can lead to the complete policy. Connect to the actual eligible order action. Do not show an active Cancel button when the backend requires support handling.

### 32 · Privacy `/policies/privacy`

**Concept:** **What we keep. What you control.**  
**Reference:** R16.

Use a brief summary of purposes and choices, then the complete approved policy with date, contents, retention, processors and contact routes. Navigation can be designed now; legal assertions require the real operating facts. Personalization should not be inferred from colour selection.

### 33 · Terms `/policies/terms`

**Concept:** **The full terms, in a readable place.**  
**Reference:** R16.

A document layout with version/date, summary, contents and full text. Link shipping/returns/cancellation rather than duplicating them inconsistently. Keep reading width comfortable and language precise. This page earns its premium quality through legibility, not theatrical motion.

### 34 · Empty, error and unavailable states

**Routes:** missing product/page, zero search results, no orders, no saved items, unavailable reports, ordering/sign-in paused; include `/preview/states` as internal QA only.  
**References:** R2’s documented empty-bag element, R14’s descriptive errors.

Use one relevant cap/bottle detail and one useful action. A 404 can say **“That colour isn’t on this shelf.”** with Shop and Home. A missing report must be direct, not cute: exact lot, what is missing and where to ask. Pending payment also needs sober language. Match the tone to the consequence.

## Shared design rules

### Colour

Keep the seven existing product colours and their mapping. A product’s colour is its identity, not a health diagnosis, goal score or status. Every coloured selector includes the product name and a stable identifier. Standard status/error/alert messaging remains readable independently of product colour.

### Type and space

Retain Aileron for fluent reading and Lemon Milk for product identity and compact labels. Use a consistent editorial headline scale across Home, About, Story and Learn. Reserve oversized display moments for a strong message; do not make every service form or long page begin with a poster. Preserve E’s generous white space while letting actual products appear earlier.

### Motion

Use three classes of motion: a signature hero sequence, short object/selection transitions, and immediate service feedback. These are proposed design budgets, not measurements: hero motion should settle quickly; control feedback should be brief enough not to delay the action. No scroll hijacking or waiting for an animation to shop. Reduced motion uses static compositions and instant state changes. Mobile selection never depends on hover.

### Imagery

Use the real bottle/cap assets and genuine brand work first. New studio renders may improve consistency, but must preserve pack artwork, proportions and product colour. Team and process photography requires authentic material. Do not reuse another brand’s imagery or reproduce another site’s composition literally.

### Content

Replace internal workflow phrases such as “supplied label artwork” in launch-facing editorial prose with concise shopper language once approved, while retaining appropriate provenance in fact/evidence sections. Do not erase pending status where the underlying record is still absent. A design review can be polished while the launch remains incomplete.

### Navigation

Give each page a distinct role: About = identity today; Story = real decisions and development; Standards = evidence; Learn = explanations. Use consistent product links between them. A long page should have a small number of strong editorial sections, with details available on demand and essential safety/price information accessible without a hunt.

## Recommended sequence for design work

1. **Hero and shared identity.** Storyboard the living fan deck, establish the first static frame, lock product-colour mapping and editorial typography. Show desktop and mobile concepts together.
2. **Home → Shop → one PDP.** Build the complete path using one product, preferably Omega-3 or Creatine because their label distinctions make the informational design testable. Then extend the shared model to all seven.
3. **About → Story → Standards.** Give these three distinct jobs. Remove empty people/founder sections until real material exists; make Standards lead with a useful record sheet.
4. **Learn publication and guide templates.** One ingredient article and one foundation article first, then apply the system to all twelve with their own diagrams.
5. **Reports and service journey.** Product/lot lookup, bag, checkout, payment recovery, order detail, account, support and policies. Preserve backend contracts and truthful availability.
6. **Finishing pass.** Motion, keyboard use, mobile forms, reading width, image performance, all empty/error states, production copy and factual sign-off.

Do not build a different spectacle for every URL. The original idea should become more recognizable as someone moves through the site.

## Material needed for the strongest execution

- Approved founder/team introduction and genuine images, if People and a founder story are desired.
- High-resolution original bottle/cap assets or the existing Blender source for controlled new compositions.
- Approved product facts, live prices and any supported benefit statements; the existing label records are the present factual baseline.
- Actual laboratory documents, publication states and relevant evidence before stronger quality visuals can make claims.
- Approved delivery, payment, returns and business details for the service pages. The visual proposal does not select those rules.

## What I would choose

Keep **“Seven supplements. One colour each.”** as the permanent idea. Use **The Living Fan Deck** as the homepage behavior, **One colour, in detail** as the product-page language, **Seven colours. One point of view** for About, and **Behind every colour, a record** for Standards.

Those four connected ideas give the site a distinctive point of view. The rest of the pages can then be quiet, useful and unmistakably E.
