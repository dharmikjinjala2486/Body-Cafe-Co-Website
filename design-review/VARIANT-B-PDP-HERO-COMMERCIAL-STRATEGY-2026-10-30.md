## Recommended opening

One recognisable product presentation, one clear benefit/value promise, one understandable offer, one primary purchase action. The customer's first impression should answer: why this product, why this price, what arrives, and what happens when I buy?

- Preserve the unchanged static first product image. Improve its surrounding stage, background, scale, shadow and whitespace rather than replacing the artwork.
- Product name plus a short approved customer-benefit headline. Limit the supporting introduction to one sentence.
- Up to three short, product-specific value points. Keep ingredient tables, research explanations and detailed comparisons below the hero.
- One compact genuine proof cue where available: actual published review aggregate, an applicable certification or a link to real evidence. Do not stack generic badges.
- Prominent total price, supporting unit/serving economics, clear offer choices and explicit bottle count.
- One dominant Add to bag action with the selected total, followed by concise verified delivery/return/purchase terms.

Desktop: roughly balanced product stage and compact purchase column. Mobile: shorter header, compact image stage, two-line message and purchase controls; persistent selected price/CTA after the purchase panel leaves view. Do not shrink text to force everything into an arbitrary first viewport. Avoid autoplay scenes delaying the buying controls.

- [Ritual multivitamin](https://ritual.com/products/essential-for-women-multivitamin): introductory offers have explicit subscription conditions; the transferable lesson is clear eligibility and future pricing, not copying a promotional percentage.
- [Baymard PDP research](https://baymard.com/research/product-page): treats images, the buy section, variation selection and shipping/returns as distinct design areas. Our proposed hierarchy is a synthesis, not a publicly established conversion uplift.

## Iron example structure

1. Static bottle stage and product title.
2. One approved benefit-led sentence and up to three short value points.
3. Pack options: 1 bottle / 2 bottles / 3 bottles. State capsules and approved servings separately.
4. Selected total is largest. Cost per capsule and saving against equivalent single-bottle purchases support it.
5. “You receive 3 standard bottles. One-time purchase.” next to the selected offer.
6. Add to bag — selected total. Concise delivery/return terms below.

Use “Lowest cost per capsule” when true; reserve “Most popular” for actual demand evidence. Let customers choose quantity deliberately. Do not frame more bottles as a medically superior dose or longer prescribed course. Keep original packaging and labels unchanged.

## Profit model and selection rules

Use net revenue excluding tax, after discounts/refunds and including any retained delivery charge. Contribution before acquisition = net revenue minus product cost, packaging, pick/pack, shipping, payment fees, gifts and expected variable return/RTO costs. Contribution after acquisition additionally subtracts attributable acquisition spend. Fixed overhead is not included, so contribution is not final accounting profit.

For an offer test, evaluate total contribution per eligible PDP visitor, not just revenue per order or add-to-bag rate. This captures both conversion and the margin of the resulting mix of orders. Over a longer window include repeat orders and credit redemption without counting them twice.

Illustration only: if a control contributes ₹400 per order and converts 3% of eligible visitors, it produces ₹12 contribution per visitor. A promotion contributing ₹320 per order needs conversion above 3.75% to improve that measure, before any other changed costs. These are hypothetical values, not BCC estimates.

For a multipack, compare against the single-bottle baseline and expected repeat purchases: a three-bottle purchase today can bring forward future orders rather than create three new purchases. Credit programmes can similarly subsidise customers who would have returned at full price.

Do not choose final discounts, gift values or free-shipping thresholds without SKU-level product costs, shipping by order weight/region, packaging, fees, margin targets and return/RTO assumptions.

## Priority and team review

First establish the common benefit-led hero hierarchy. Prototype Iron's value ladder, a full-price benefit/creative treatment and one margin-costed added-value offer. Retain the other ideas as distinct candidates for team review.

The team should assess whether the product feels desirable, its value is obvious, contents and total are clear, and the offer is credible. Real commercial validation later requires same-product comparisons with comparable traffic, tracking completed orders, conversion, net AOV, contribution per visitor, returns/RTO, repeat purchases and support complaints. Seven different products are not a controlled A/B test.

Existing approved product facts, images after the first, evidence and stock remain admin/backend-owned. New offer prices, eligibility, gifts and credits require validated admin configuration and server-side enforcement. The creative layout and motion remain code-owned. No large educational widgets should crowd the initial purchase decision.
