CHAPTER 03 · THE FORMULA

What goes into the formula

The front of a pack names the headline ingredient. The formula is everything else you need to compare two products fairly: what each ingredient is, in what form, how much, and what comes with it.

WHAT EACH FIELD TELLS YOU
1
Ingredient, in full
The ingredient and its form, such as “Iron (as iron bisglycinate)”. The form is part of the ingredient's identity.
2
Amount per serving
Stated with its unit (mg, mcg or IU), and kept separate from the weight of the compound where the two differ.
3
Serving size
How many capsules, softgels or chews make one serving. Every amount refers to this.
4
Units per pack
How many units the pack holds, so you know how many servings you're buying.
5
Other ingredients
Capsule or softgel shell, fillers, flavours and anything else added, and what each one is there to do.
6
Allergens
For example fish, which matters for Omega-3 Fish Oil.
7
Directions
How much to take, how often and how.
8
Cautions and storage
Who should take extra care, and how to keep the product.
The everyday collection · Studio product images.
HOW TO COMPARE TWO PRODUCTS
01
Compare per serving, not per pack.

Two packs of the same size can hold very different servings.

02
Match the form, not just the name.

Different compounds of one nutrient deliver different amounts of it.

03
Convert before comparing.

For vitamin D, 1 mcg equals 40 IU, according to NIH's Office of Dietary Supplements.

04
Read the actives, not the total.

With fish oil, EPA and DHA matter more than the total amount of oil.

CHAPTER 08 · HOW WE COMMUNICATE

How we communicate responsibly

How we say something matters as much as what we say. These are the practices already in place across this site.

HOW IT WORKS
01
Sources are cited, and linked.

Technical points in our guides are limited to what their linked primary sources state, such as NIH Office of Dietary Supplements fact sheets and the FSSAI health supplement regulations.

02
Missing facts are marked, not filled.

Where an approved label or document hasn't arrived, the page says so instead of estimating.

03
No claims we can't support.

No health claims, invented reviews, ratings, endorsements or badges.

THREE KINDS OF STATEMENT, KEPT APART
Ingredient research
What studies found about an ingredient. Background, not a promise about our product.
Product testing
What a test of a sample from one of our lots showed. None published yet.
Customer experience
What customers tell us. Reviews come only from delivered orders, after moderation. None published yet.
WHAT YOU CAN CHECK

Every guide lists its sources at the end. Read a label with confidence is a good place to start.
