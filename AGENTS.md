Variant E Part 2 (`?design=e&part=2`, "Creative") and Part 3 (`?design=e&part=3`, "Minimal") are complete. Preserve their presentation.

Variant E "Part 2 · Content Focus" (`?design=e&part=5`) is a working duplicate of Part 2 for content changes. It runs as Part 2 (`body[data-variant-e-part="2"]`, same files, data and connected store) and adds `body[data-content-focus="1"]`; scope anything specific to it under that attribute so Part 2 stays as it is. Its new copy (from the client's Version E Copy Transformation workbook) lives in `web/content-focus-e.js` and `web/content-focus-e.css`.

Content Focus content review (16 Nov 2026, owner-approved page by page): every page was checked against the final labels, the Version E workbook and its claims sheet, and the store's own policies. Rules it follows: a product's use is only its front-label Key Benefit; "Take what you need. Skip what you don't."; testing is "from our first production batch" with no laboratory named until agreements are signed; the founder's title "Scientific Founder" is not used until approved. `web/content-focus-e.js` rewrites each page as it renders and, for the parts that redraw themselves afterwards (the bag, batch lookup results, the search box), re-applies its changes from one `MutationObserver` on `#app`; each step is safe to run twice. Its rules that must beat Part 2's later style sheets are written `body[data-variant-e-part="2"][data-content-focus="1"] …`. The site's manufacturer-name filter (`MAKER` in `web/part2-e.js`) drops any sentence containing "manufactur", so new copy avoids the word ("Mfg. Date", "developing medicines").

The Content Focus product page copy is decided in the admin portal (16 Nov 2026): the product form's "Content Focus copy" section holds `cf_claim` and `cf_facts` ("What it's for"), `cf_headline`, `cf_support`, `cf_ingredient`, `cf_form`, `cf_amount`, `cf_decision` (Description) and `cf_points` ("At a glance", one per line; "One bottle lasts … days" is still worked out from the servings). `web/content-focus-e.js` lays these over its `PRODUCTS` and `USE` tables before every page renders (`fromAdmin`), so the headline also reaches the range, Learn and Our standard pages; a blank field hides its piece. The tables in the code stay as the defaults the admin form starts from (`window.BCCContentFocusCopy.defaults`, merged in `web/label-content.js`); the shop-card and range use lines are still only in the code. The field limits are listed twice and must stay in step: `TEXT_FIELDS` in `app/product_content.py` and `eLimits` in `supabase/functions/operations/core.js`. Part 2 itself keeps its own copy from the code.

The same review changed shared files, for every design: the five policies are Version 1.1 (a policy in `web/support-content.js` may carry its own `version` and `updated`); five product answers were added to the Help library (run `node deploy/build_chat_knowledge.mjs` after editing it; the live Barista only learns them when the Supabase function is redeployed); E–H search also matches the label claim and the veg / non-veg mark; the preview account's sample orders open through `window.BCCPreviewAccount` (`web/part2-account-e.js`); and the lot→batch swap leaves "a lot to say" alone and covers the search box.

Part 2's inner pages (Our standard, Learn index, Story, Help index, Look up a batch) and its finishing touches on the bag, checkout and sign-in live in `web/part2-pages-e.js` + `web/part2-pages-e.css` (12 Nov 2026), scoped to `body[data-variant-e-part="2"]` with the `p2p-` class prefix (`pp-` belongs to the older product page). They replace the shared pages for Part 2 and Content Focus only; Parts 3 and 4 keep the versions in `web/part2-e.js`. The same files dress Contact, the five policy pages and Preferences for Part 2 (class `p2p-su`): those keep the shared markup and behaviour from `web/support.js` and gain a policy switcher, a scroll-tracking contents card and Part 2 styling. Their sentences are kept whole because `web/content-focus-e.js` swaps copy by exact text and reads `.p2-std-status`, `.p2-rules` and `.p2-actions-left`.

Product page galleries in E Parts 1–4 and Content Focus (16 Nov 2026) are decided in the admin portal: each product page shows the images marked "Show on website" in the product's Images list, in the admin's order (`window.BCCGalleryE.images(p)` in `web/gallery-e.js`). The owner's seven-image listing set, `web/media/gallery/<product-id>-01 … -07.webp` (1254 × 1254, converted from the "Amazon Listing images" folders), was put into every product's admin list the same day; the entries point at those site files (the admin API accepts `/web/media/…` paths as well as uploads), and the set stays in `web/gallery-e.js` with its labels and alt text as the fallback for a product whose admin list is empty. Image 1 is the bottle; image 7 is the selected label facts drawn as a panel, not a rear-label photograph. The panels carry text, so gallery images are always shown whole (`object-fit: contain`) in a square frame. Part 4 shows the gallery on the cap-colour block in place of the turning bottle. To change a picture, upload or reorder it in the admin portal and publish.

Apply new design revisions only to Variant E Part 4 (`?design=e&part=4`, "The Café"): the brand is a café that serves seven things and says exactly what is in each. Its own chrome, nothing visual shared with Parts 1–3: Anton display type, the seven cap colours as full-bleed blocks, the bottle stills and cap close-ups (`web/media/premium/`) filling the screen, menu lines with dot leaders. The mechanic is Service (Morning · Noon · Night): a header switch, stored in `sessionStorage` and applied as `body[data-p4-service]`, that changes the palette (paper / white / ink) and, from each label's `label_timing`, which menu items stand forward; product pages open in the service their label says is best. Café words are limited to: Now serving, Service, Menu, House rules, Regulars, House notes, Order number, Opening hours, Not on the menu. Nothing may suggest taking all seven. Same content, flow, data and backend as Parts 2 and 3. Part 4 lives in `web/part4-e.js` + `web/part4-e.css` (scoped to `body[data-variant-e-part="4"]`) and uses the shared Part 2 layer (`web/part2-e.js`, `web/part2-e.css`, which serve Parts 2, 3 and 4) for data helpers, bottles, spins and the add / lot-picker / filter / frequently-bought-with behaviours; a change to the shared files must leave Parts 2 and 3 looking the same. Keep Part 1 as the production design unless the user explicitly requests a promotion. No label photographs anywhere in E (the rear labels print the manufacturer).

The store assistant (12 Nov 2026) is an AI chat for Part 2 and Content Focus only: `web/chat-e.js` + `web/chat-e.css` (prefix `p2c-`, scoped to `body[data-variant-e-part="2"]`). The assistant is called Barista; its character is the cream bottle in an indigo cap and apron (`web/media/barista/`, drawn for the site, no label on the body). It posts the conversation to `POST /api/chat`, which calls Claude (`claude-opus-5-5`) and streams the answer back as plain text: `supabase/functions/operations/chat.js` on the live site, `app/chat.py` in the local preview (it reads its rules, limits and knowledge from the hosted files, so edit those). What the assistant knows about the shop is the live catalogue plus `chat-knowledge.js`, generated from `web/support-content.js`; after changing that file run `node deploy/build_chat_knowledge.mjs`. The API key is the `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` secret (function secret when hosted, environment variable locally) and never goes in the code or the browser. Its rules keep the site's own: store facts only from our content, no personal medical advice, never suggest all seven, never name the manufacturer.

Online payment (13 Nov 2026) is Razorpay Standard Checkout, on the shared payment page (`/payment/:id`, drawn by `web/part2-e.js` for Parts 2, 3, 4 and Content Focus). Pay asks `POST /api/orders/:id/pay` for a Razorpay order (its id is recorded on our order as `rzp_order` so Pay reuses it; the receipt, our order number, is the fallback search, which Razorpay updates about half a minute late), opens Razorpay's window, and sends its signed answer to `POST /api/orders/:id/pay/verify`; only the API marks an order paid, after checking the signature and reading the Razorpay order back (receipt, amount, a successful payment). `app/razorpay.py` in the local preview, `supabase/functions/operations/razorpay.js` plus the pay routes in `core.js` on the live site; keep the two in step. The keys are `RAZORPAY_KEY_ID` and `RAZORPAY_KEY_SECRET` (git-ignored `.env` locally, function secrets when hosted); test keys (`rzp_test_…`) take no money, and the secret never goes in the code or the browser. Checkout's window closes 30 seconds before the 15-minute stock hold ends; a payment that still lands after the hold is recorded on the order (`payment_after_hold`) for a person to ship or refund, never marked paid. Webhooks are not set up yet (go-live step). Tests: `app/test_razorpay.py`, `deploy/supabase-tests/test-razorpay.mjs`.

The hosted build joins files (13 Nov 2026): `deploy/build_netlify.py` writes each run of neighbouring style sheets and classic scripts in `web/index.html` as one file in `dist/web/b/`, named by its content and cached for a year (`vercel.json`). Order and contents are unchanged, and the local preview still loads the separate files, so keep adding files to `web/index.html` as before. A script that opens with a top-level `"use strict"` is left on its own.
