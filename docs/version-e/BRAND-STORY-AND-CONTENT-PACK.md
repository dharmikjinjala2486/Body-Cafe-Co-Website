## 1. Master brand story

*755 words. Publishable as written.*

### Ask what's in it

Watch anyone order at a good café. They ask questions without thinking about it. Is there egg in this? How sweet is it? Can I have it without the cream? Nobody behind the counter is offended, and nobody thinks the customer is being difficult. The menu has already told them most of it, and whatever it hasn't, someone will.

We do this with almost everything we eat and drink. We turn the biscuit packet over to look for the green dot. We ask whether the mithai is made in ghee. We read the menu before we order, not after. Asking what's in something is one of the most ordinary, sensible habits we have.

Then we come to supplements, things many of us take every single day for months, and the habit quietly disappears.

It isn't that people stop caring. It's that the category rarely makes room for the question. Supplement shopping tends to ask for trust first and offer detail later, if at all. A large number on the front of the pack that sometimes measures the oil rather than the omega-3 inside it, or the weight of a whole compound rather than the mineral. A blend that never shows its individual amounts. A badge with no document behind it. A promise about how you'll feel.

So the careful buyer does the work alone: a screenshot of the back label, a search late at night, a message forwarded by a cousin who read something somewhere. Many give up and buy whatever looks familiar. The ones who keep asking are made to feel like the problem.

We think it should work the other way round.

Body Café Co. is built on one belief: before you take something every day, you should be able to know exactly what it is. Not a vague sense of it. The actual ingredient, in the form it actually takes, in the amount that matters, with the record of the batch in your hand.

That's why we're called a café. There's no café to visit. The name is a standard. A good counter treats a question as normal, and its menu tells you before you order. Detail first. Trust after, earned.

So every product we make follows the same few rules.

We name the form, because the form is part of the ingredient. Iron bisglycinate is not simply "iron". Vitamin K2 comes in more than one form, so we say which one. Omega-3 as triglycerides. Psyllium husk, the isabgol you already know. Said plainly, on the front.

We state the number that matters: the amount of the thing itself, per serving, with the serving beside it. Where two ingredients share a bottle, you see two amounts, never one combined total.

We put the routine in plain words: how much, when, and with what, next to the facts rather than hidden behind a link.

We show the record. Every pack carries a lot number that leads to the record for the batch it was made in. When a document isn't ready, we say so, instead of filling the gap with a badge.

And we give each product its own cap colour, so the range is easy to read from across a room, or across a kitchen shelf at seven in the morning.

What we won't do matters just as much. We won't promise how you'll feel. We won't invent reviews, endorsements or certificates. We won't rush you with countdowns or "only three left". A light tone is welcome here; a blurred fact is not.

None of this is meant to make supplements feel clinical. Most of life with them is small and ordinary: a bottle on the shelf beside the tea tin, a cap colour you recognise half-awake, a routine that sticks because it's simple. The detail is there for the moments you want it. When a doctor asks what you're taking. When a friend asks whether it's worth it. When you're holding two bottles and want to compare like with like.

Our first collection is seven everyday supplements, from iron to isabgol. There will be more, in new forms and new formats, made wherever the right ingredient is made. The rules won't change. Name it. State it. Explain it. Show the record.

Curiosity is a good place to start. It's how we choose what we eat, what we cook and who we trust. It deserves a place in what we take every day, too.

So ask what's in it. You should never have to wonder.

**Body Café Co. A little more knowing.**

## 3. Brand architecture

**Master belief →** Before you take something every day, you should be able to know exactly what it is.

**Brand idea →** The café counter: a question is normal, and the menu comes before the order. *Ask what's in it.*

**Master tagline →** A little more knowing.

### Tagline decision

| Line | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Built on research. Made for real life. | **Retire as master tagline** (may stay as an internal brief line) | "Research" is a claim the site itself says isn't evidenced yet: Learn guides are pending specialist review and no finished-product tests are published. It's the most common line in the category ("science-backed", "research-led"), so it builds no distinct memory. It doesn't use the café idea or the cap colours. |
| **A little more knowing.** | **Adopt** | Already a Version E asset (Learn hero) and used in A/B, so recognition has started. Makes no outcome claim. Warm, modest, easy in Indian English. Works for any future product, format or origin. |
| Ask what's in it. | **Adopt as the signature / call to curiosity** | Short, active, ties directly to the café and the label. Used as the homepage H1, social sign-off and Amazon headline. |

### Five message pillars

| # | Pillar | What it means | Proof we can show today | What it never becomes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Name it properly** | The form is part of the ingredient's name. | Bisglycinate, MK-7, triglycerides, monohydrate, isabgol printed on labels. | "Superior form" or absorption claims without attribution. |
| 2 | **The number that matters** | The amount of the active itself, per serving, with the serving beside it. Two ingredients, two amounts. | Elemental iron, omega-3 vs oil, myo and D-chiro listed separately. | Comparisons with named competitors. |
| 3 | **Fits the day** | How much, when and with what, in plain words, next to the facts. | Directions on every label. | Promises of results from a routine. |
| 4 | **Show the record** | Every pack → its lot → its record. Say what isn't ready. | Lot lookup (demonstration), honest status ledger. | "Tested" badges without published documents. |
| 5 | **Questions welcome** | The café counter: no question is silly; answers come before you buy. | Support cases with IDs, Learn guides, glossary, product Q&A. | Personal medical advice. |

### Product selection philosophy (future-proof filter)

A product joins the collection when we can answer five questions on its label:

1. **What is it?** An everyday ingredient people already ask about.
2. **Which form?** A form we can name in full.
3. **How much?** An amount of the active itself we can state per serving.
4. **How does it fit the day?** Directions simple enough to keep.
5. **Can we show the record?** A lot number and a batch record we can link to.

*Stress test:* Magnesium Glycinate (form named: glycinate; elemental magnesium stated) ✓ · Beetroot extract (standardised nitrate % named; source of the % attributed) ✓ · a gummy or powder (routine pillar) ✓ · a blend (each component's amount shown, never "proprietary") ✓ · an ingredient from another country (record shows origin; no "imported is better") ✓ · a product for another life stage (no demographic in the belief) ✓.

### Tone of voice

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Plain English first; explain a term the first time. | Jargon to sound clever, or dumbing down. |
| Specific: name the form, the unit, the serving. | "Advanced formula", "clinically proven", "pure", "potent". |
| Warm and a little playful (café, cap colours, kitchen shelf). | Jokes about health, bodies or fear. |
| Say what's missing: "not published yet", "still being approved". | Fill gaps with claims or vague reassurance. |
| Short sentences, active voice, Indian English spelling. | Countdown, scarcity or guilt language. |
| Invite questions: "Ask us", "Ask what's in it". | Personal medical advice, or "this will make you feel…". |

Test every line: *Would a good café counter say it, and could we show the record behind it?*

### Product storytelling framework (every SKU, current and future)

1. **Line** (≤ 9 words): ingredient named properly + the one detail we make plain.
2. **The question to ask first:** the single label detail that matters most for this ingredient.
3. **Form:** the full name, and why the form is part of the identity.
4. **The number that matters:** amount of the active per serving, serving size beside it (from the approved label only).
5. **Fits the day:** directions in plain words.
6. **The record:** maker named on the pack, lot lookup, what's published.
7. **What we don't say:** the claims we leave out, and why.
