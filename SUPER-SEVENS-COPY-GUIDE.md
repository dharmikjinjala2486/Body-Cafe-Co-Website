# The Super Sevens copy guide

**Status:** draft for owner approval, 17 Nov 2026. Nothing on the site has changed yet.
**Applies to:** every word in Variant E Content Focus (`?design=e&part=5`): headings, descriptions, buttons, forms, messages, FAQs, policies, SMS / WhatsApp / email, page titles, alt text and the Barista.
**How to use it:** before a line goes on the site, it should pass the checklist in section 9. If a line and this guide disagree, the line changes (or the guide does, with the owner's say-so).

Facts in this guide come from `web/label-records.json` (the final labels) and the owner-approved Content Focus copy in `web/content-focus-e.js` (16 Nov 2026). Prices are left out because they change in the admin portal.

---

## 1. The idea

**In one line**
> The Super Sevens: seven supplements, each with its own cap colour, its own reason, and every amount on the front.

**In five words** (for tight spaces): *Seven supplements. Every amount printed.*

**In 40 words** (for an intro or the About page):
> We make seven supplements, and only seven. Five are single ingredients; two are deliberate pairs. Each has its own cap colour, its form named on the front and its amount printed per serving. Take what you need. Skip what you don't.

**The brand line, kept:** *No ingredient without a reason.*

### What "Super Sevens" never means
These are the existing house rules from the Super Sevens page. Every page follows them.
1. It doesn't mean the products are superior, stronger or clinically better.
2. It isn't a seven-day plan or a reason to take all seven. "Take what you need. Skip what you don't." appears on every page that shows the whole range.
3. Nicknames come from facts on the label (format, pack size, directions). They never describe effects.
4. The label on your pack comes first; the site repeats it.

So: no "collect all seven", "complete your set", "the full seven", "your daily seven", bundle savings or anything that counts how many a customer owns.

## 2. What every page has to prove

Each of the seven answers the same four questions. These are the approved "four decisions", and they are the backbone of every description we write.

| Decision | The question | What the copy shows |
|---|---|---|
| Ingredient | Is this the right ingredient? | Chosen for the purpose, never to lengthen a label. |
| Form | Is this the right form? | The form, named in full (Ferrochel® bisglycinate, MK-7, triglycerides). |
| Amount | Is this the right amount? | The amount you actually get, per serving, against a reference where there is one. |
| Company | Does it belong with anything else? | On its own, or paired for a stated reason. |

A good description answers at least one of these with a number from the label. A line that answers none of them is decoration; cut it.

## 3. The voice

| We are | That sounds like | Not like |
|---|---|---|
| **Plain** | "The iron you actually get, not the weight of the compound." | "Advanced bioavailable mineral complex." |
| **Exact** | "800 mg of omega-3 in every softgel." | "High-strength omega-3." |
| **Warm and Indian-everyday** | "Morning, empty stomach. Chai two hours later." | "Consume pre-prandially." |
| **Calm and confident** | "We print the sugar too: 3.6 g per serving." | "Guilt-free! Zero compromise!" |

**Rhythm:** short sentences, often in pairs ("Iron first. Chai later."). Average under 15 words. One idea per sentence.
**Person:** "we" for Body Café Co., "you" for the reader. Never "users" or "consumers".
**Tone at hard moments** (errors, payment, delivery problems): calm and specific. Say what happened, what is safe, what to do next. No jokes.

## 4. Words

### Reach for
exact · printed · per serving · on the front · named · chosen · measured · on its own · paired · the amount you actually get · as the label directs · Batch No. · cap colour · take what you need

### Never use
| Word or idea | Why |
|---|---|
| best, #1, ultimate, superior, strongest, most powerful | Superiority claims; also against house rule 1 |
| clinically proven, doctor-recommended, guaranteed, results | Claims we can't support on the site |
| boost, detox, cleanse, miracle, superfood, hack | Hype; implies effects beyond the label |
| cure, treat, prevent, fix, heal, or any disease or condition name | Medicinal claims; the labels say "Not for medicinal use" |
| collect, complete, full set, all seven, daily seven | House rule 2 |
| manufacturer, manufacturing, manufactured | The site filter drops the whole sentence. Use "Mfg. Date", "developing medicines", "made" |
| lot (to customers) | Customers see "batch" and "Batch No." |
| Scientific Founder | Not approved yet. Use "Dr. Nayan Solanki (PhD, Industrial Pharmacy), the formulation scientist behind our seven" |
| any laboratory name | Not until agreements are signed. Use "from our first production batch, independent laboratories test each batch" |

### Style sheet
- **Product names** exactly as printed: Iron Bisglycinate, L-Theanine, Vitamin D3 + K2, Omega-3 Fish Oil, Myo & D-Chiro Inositol, Creatine Chews, Psyllium Husk.
- **Numbers:** always figures with units: 29 mg, 600 IU, 55 mcg, 3,000 mg (comma from 1,000), 40:1. Write "3 g" only where the label number (3,000 mg) also appears nearby.
- **Money:** ₹1,499, ₹75. "MRP, inclusive of all taxes."
- **Spelling:** Indian/British (colour, fibre, flavour) in our sentences; quote the label exactly as printed (its front says "Fiber Supplement").
- **Headings:** sentence case, ending with a full stop. **Buttons and links:** no full stop.
- **Punctuation:** curly apostrophes (’), en dash for ranges (3–5 g), middle dot to join short facts (29 mg iron · 60 capsules).

### How the seven talk about each other
- **Compare facts, never rank.** "The only chewable of the seven" is a fact. "Our most popular" is not ours to say.
- **Suggesting another product:** "Also in the range" / "Add one only if you need it. Each has its own timing, so follow every label." Never "goes great with", "completes", or "works better together", except for the two deliberate pairs, which are inside one product.
- **Timing words** come from each label's `label_timing`. Six are "best in the morning"; Psyllium is "best at night".

## 7. Rules for each kind of text

| Kind | Length | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Heading | ≤ 8 words | a fact, a contrast or a promise we can keep | "Count the omega-3, not the oil." |
| Intro under a heading | ≤ 25 words | widen the heading with one number | "1,000 mg of anchovy oil per softgel, carrying 800 mg of omega-3." |
| Description / body | ≤ 35 words a paragraph | what it is → why it was chosen → the number | "Iron stands alone. Calcium in the same meal can reduce how much iron is absorbed, so the directions keep milk and chai two hours away." |
| Bullet ("At a glance") | ≤ 10 words | one fact each, starting with the number | "29 mg elemental iron per capsule" |
| Button | 1–4 words | a plain verb; core actions never get clever | "Add to bag", "See what's inside", "Look up a batch" |
| Form label | 1–3 words | the thing, not an instruction | "Mobile number" |
| Helper text | ≤ 15 words | why we ask, or the format | "We'll send your order updates here." |
| Error | ≤ 25 words | what happened → what's safe → what to do | "We couldn't open the payment window. Please try again." |
| Empty state | ≤ 20 words | turn the dead end into a next step | "Nothing in your bag yet. Find the one you came for, by its cap colour." |
| FAQ answer | first sentence answers | answer, then the detail, then a link | — |
| SMS | ≤ 160 characters, plain characters (no é, no ₹, no curly quotes) | brand, order number, one fact, one link | "Body Cafe Co.: order ##order## has shipped with ##courier##, tracking number ##awb##. ##link##" (the drafted template in `MSG91-TEMPLATES.md`) |
| Page title (browser / Google) | ≤ 60 characters | product + signature number + brand | "Iron Bisglycinate · 29 mg per capsule · Body Café Co." |
| Google description | ≤ 155 characters | the ID card's first three lines | — |
| Image alt text | ≤ 15 words | what's shown, cap colour included | "Iron Bisglycinate bottle with its rust-coloured cap" |

Say "Nothing was charged" (or anything else about the customer's money) only where the code path makes it true.

## 9. Checklist for every line

1. Does it answer a real question, or prove one of the four decisions?
2. Is there a number from the label where one would help?
3. Is the "What it's for" wording the front label's Key Benefit, word for word, and nothing more?
4. Is every fact in `label-records.json` or approved copy?
5. No word from the "Never use" list, no condition or disease names, no "manufactur…"?
6. Nothing that suggests taking several, or all seven?
7. Testing wording is "from our first production batch", with no laboratory named?
8. Within the length for its kind (section 7)?
9. Read aloud: does it sound like a person behind a café counter, not a brochure?
10. Would it still make sense to someone reading only this line on a phone?

## 10. Decisions for the owner

1. **The one-liner** in section 1: approve, or choose another.
2. **Cap colour names** (Rust, Olive, Indigo, Slate, Lilac, Deep Teal, Sand): use them in copy, or keep colours unnamed.
3. **L-Theanine nickname:** keep "The Thirty", or change it to "The Green Tea One".
4. **Creatine timing:** its label directions say "choose one time, morning, noon or night", but its timing record says "best in the morning" (Part 4's Service switch uses that record). Which should the site follow?
5. **"Never say" lists:** add anything the FSSAI consultant flags.
